REAL SEO OUTPUT COMPARISON

I Compared the SEO Structure of 6 Shopify AI Blogging Apps Using Real Published Articles

Instead of comparing feature lists, I looked at the actual SEO structure of published Shopify blog posts associated with autoBlogger, Bloggle, Essential AI SEO, Auto Blogs Agent, Jolt and AI Blog Agent.

By Ollie Hermans · 18 September 2026

There are plenty of Shopify apps that promise to generate SEO blog content.

But an app saying it creates “SEO-optimised articles” does not necessarily tell you much about the final page.

What I wanted to know was:

What does the actual SEO output look like once the article is published on a Shopify store?

So I looked at real published articles associated with six Shopify blogging apps and compared things such as:

  • Semantic H2/H3 structure
  • Article depth and word count
  • Search-intent coverage
  • Table of contents
  • Key takeaways and summaries
  • Internal and product linking
  • FAQ sections
  • Tables and lists
  • Author and trust signals
  • Source attribution
  • Images
  • Structured, extractable answers
  • How easily an LLM or AI search engine could understand the page

I build autoBlogger, so I obviously have an interest in the result. That is also why I wanted to make the methodology transparent and link directly to every article used.

How I selected the articles

Shopify blog posts generally do not include public metadata identifying which app created them. For the competing apps, I used a simple assumption: if a merchant publicly reviewed a Shopify blogging app and was publishing blog posts around the same period, it is reasonable to assume they were using the blogging functionality they were reviewing.

In several cases, the merchant review explicitly says the app was writing or publishing their blog posts.

That still is not absolute proof of authorship.

The autoBlogger article is different because I can directly confirm that it was created with autoBlogger. For the other apps, I treat the selected pages as likely examples based on merchant review and publication timing.

If any provider included here believes the selected article was not created or assisted by their app, they are welcome to contact me with a confirmed public example and I will update the comparison.

The SEO results at a glance

I used two scores.

SEO score looks at traditional on-page SEO, including semantic headings, search intent, depth, internal links, structured content, trust signals and general article architecture.

LLM-readiness score looks at how easily an AI search or answer system can understand and extract useful information from the page.

These are my own comparison scores, not Google scores or scores from a third-party SEO platform.

Why the structure matters

Instead of seeing one long article body, a crawler can understand that the page contains separate sections about diamond quality, craftsmanship techniques, technology, certification, selecting a jeweller and other related subtopics.

The table of contents makes the topical architecture explicit. The key-takeaway section creates answer-first content. The FAQ expands the number of follow-up queries the page can answer. The glossary explicitly defines important terms and entities.

That is useful for traditional search, but it is also particularly strong for AI systems trying to break a page into understandable, reusable chunks.

What mattered most for SEO

1. Semantic headings matter

A page can look beautifully structured while exposing a much flatter document structure underneath. Automated blogging tools should be generating proper semantic HTML by default.

2. Length only helps when structure supports it

The Essential AI SEO article has plenty of content, but without the same level of semantic organisation, some of that value is harder for crawlers and AI systems to interpret.

3. SEO articles should create multiple entry points

H2s, H3s, FAQs, tables, definitions, lists and key takeaways each create another clearly defined piece of information on the same page.

4. LLM optimisation overlaps heavily with good SEO

Clear headings, direct answers, named entities, definitions, sources, tables, FAQs, author information and useful internal links help both traditional SEO and AI understanding.

Why autoBlogger scored highest

The autoBlogger article was not simply the longest article.

It scored highest because it combined more SEO elements on the same page:

  • Proper H2/H3 structure

  • Internal context

This is also why I do not really think of autoBlogger as just an “AI writer” anymore.

Writing the paragraphs is only one stage. The bigger goal is to automate the entire Shopify blogging and SEO workflow around those paragraphs.

So which had the strongest SEO output?

In this particular real-world sample, autoBlogger produced the strongest overall SEO structure.

SEO: 92/100
LLM-readiness: 95/100

Bloggle's example had particularly strong sourcing and editorial authority, although Bloggle is not directly comparable as an AI writer.

Essential AI SEO produced plenty of content but was held back by surprisingly weak semantic heading structure.

Auto Blogs Agent had better basic headings but significantly less depth.

Jolt and AI Blog Agent produced much thinner pages in the examples I found.

This does not prove that every article generated by autoBlogger will outperform every article generated by another platform. It is a comparison of six real published examples.

But inspecting the actual SEO output is a lot more informative than comparing feature lists that all say roughly the same thing.
